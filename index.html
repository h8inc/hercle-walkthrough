Low-fidelity on purpose. the Blockstream app is a self-custody wallet; Hercle is the regulated VASP behind the cash account. Everything solid is what we intend to build; everything dashed amber is a question we need answered before we can commit the flow. Click a step to walk its screens. First release scope: steps 0–4 plus withdraw — if users can deposit cash they must be able to withdraw it without buying. Only sell targets a later release.

The Blockstream app stays a self-custody wallet and owns 100% of the interface. Hercle is the account of record behind it: EUR cash balance, execution & settlement, fiat rails, and VASP compliance. The user never leaves our app — and crypto never accumulates at Hercle: every buy executes and auto-withdraws to the user's own wallet.

What we assume

  • API-first integration: the user contracts with Hercle (T&C) but all UI, comms and support entry points live in our app.
  • Hercle holds only fiat for the user, plus crypto transiently during settlement.
  • REST + webhooks, with a sandbox we can build against.

  3. 0.3 We'd be your first consumer-wallet integration — which parts of this walkthrough don't exist on your side yet?

The user opts in from a promo card, sees what the cash account is (and who provides it), and we create the account via API. The core unknown: what data you need, and when — we'd like account creation to be near-zero friction, deferring everything possible to the KYC step.

What we assume

  • Creation needs little more than email + country of residence; identity data arrives with KYC.
  • We present Hercle's terms in-app; acceptance is recorded and passed to you.
  • The user never sees a Hercle login — the account is reachable only through our app.

  2. 1.2 OUR INTENDED MODEL (pending Dom's sign-off) — silent creation via API; no Hercle credentials, no Hercle-sent emails; all comms through our app. Can you make that happen — and if not, where exactly does it break?
  3. 1.3 Which agreements must the user accept, and can we present them in-app on your behalf?
  4. 1.4 Supported countries: we don't want to geolocate everyone just to gate the entry point. Our proposal: onboarding simply states who can access (“available in … — more countries coming soon”). Which jurisdictions are live at launch?

  6. 1.6 Account deletion: we must offer a “delete account” action. We can delete our profile and the Sumsub applicant — how does deleting user data work on your side, and what must you retain as a VASP (and for how long)?

We already integrate Sumsub for verification. The user verifies once, in our app; we pass the result to you so the cash account activates for funding and withdrawal. This step is well defined as long as we generate the links and operate our own Sumsub account: once the user completes the flow, we trigger applicant sharing via Sumsub token sharing so Hercle can access the applicant and run their compliance checks.

What we assume

  • One KYC, done in-app on our Sumsub account; on completion we trigger applicant sharing to Hercle via Sumsub token sharing.
  • KYC level maps to limits (fund / buy / withdraw) that we surface in the UI.
  • Status changes reach us by webhook so the app reflects them live.

  2. 2.2 After the share: you run your own compliance checks on the applicant — does anything else need to travel outside Sumsub?

  6. 2.6 Rejections and edge cases (PEP hit, name mismatch) — whose support owns the user?

The user wires euros to their cash account and sees the balance appear inside our app — ideally with a push notification the moment funds credit. That requires a webhook from you on credit. Rails are settled: SEPA only at launch — the open items are the credit webhook and the operational details of the wire.

Buys initiate in our app against your execution/liquidity, and settle by auto-withdrawing to the user's own wallet — a fresh address per buy. That makes address registration (travel rule) the crux. Our model: the user consents once per wallet ("link"); on every buy the app derives the next address, signs an ownership proof itself, and registers it with you via API — silent for software wallets. Hardware (Jade) needs on-device approval per signature, so that path differs.

What we assume

  • Registration is per receive address (never xpub — we won't share whole-wallet history).
  • Because the app is the wallet, cryptographic ownership proof is signed in-app — no Satoshi test, nothing for the user to paste.
  • We link at first buy regardless of amount, so the €1k threshold never surprises anyone mid-flow.
  • Fresh address per buy — we refuse address reuse on privacy grounds.

For Hercle

  1. 4.1 PROPOSAL — we use your RFQ with a locked quote; validity window is an API param (15 s default). Confirm, and confirm partial fills can't happen on RFQ.
  2. 4.2 Fee structure on buys — your fee/spread, and can our fee be layered into the same quote?
  3. 4.3 Registering an address: what exactly do you need — a signed message per address, in what format? Does our signature satisfy verification alone, or do you run a second method (analytics)? Anything user-visible?
  4. 4.4 Can we pre-register batches of future addresses? Caps, rate limits, expiry on unused ones? Is registration synchronous — can a buy proceed while it's pending, or is it a hard gate?
  5. 4.5 (ours to solve, not Hercle's) Hardware wallets: Jade requires on-device approval per signature. Our design call: batch-register at link time vs. an alternative attestation path. Kept here for completeness.
  6. 4.6 PARTLY ANSWERED — network fees are constant per network (you keep a fee table) and the user pays them from their BTC balance. Asks: share the fee table, confirm the user pays the withdrawal fee in BTC, and how soon after execution the withdrawal broadcasts / whether you batch.
  7. 4.7 The €1,000 threshold — applied per transfer or aggregated? Do you verify all self-hosted withdrawals regardless of amount as internal policy?
  8. 4.8 Can a registration be rejected (analytics flag)? Once verified, is an address whitelisted permanently (EBA GL 86) — or does KYC re-verification invalidate it?

The reverse trip: the user sends bitcoin from their wallet to you, you screen it (chain analytics, travel rule on incoming), and the proceeds land in the cash balance. The open design question is price risk: can the price lock at initiation, or is it sold at market only after confirmations + screening?

What we assume

  • The app builds and sends the deposit transaction itself — no copy-pasting addresses.
  • A sell from an already-linked wallet should be low-friction: you already know these addresses are the user's.
  • Screening happens on your side; we surface honest states (sent → screening → credited), never a silent hold.

For Hercle

  1. 5.1 Deposit address for sells: static per user, rotating, or per transaction?
  2. 5.2 As the originating (self-hosted) wallet — what travel-rule data must we attach to the transfer? Does a wallet already linked for buys count as pre-cleared for sells, or is every incoming transfer screened fresh?
  3. 5.3 Confirmations required before credit, typical screening/hold time, and what happens when a transfer is flagged — user comms, whose support?
  4. 5.4 Price protection: lock a quote at initiation, or market-sell after the deposit credits? If locked — for how long, and what if confirmations run late?

The user moves euros from the cash balance to their bank account. Simple on the surface — the questions are about destination rules (name match, verification, first-time destinations) and whether anything faster than plain SEPA exists. In scope for v1: a user who deposits and never buys must be able to get their money back.

What we assume

  • SEPA payout to an IBAN in the user's own name; saved destinations after first use.
  • Webhook on completion so the app can close the loop.

For Hercle

  1. 6.1 Payout rails: SEPA only? SEPA Instant? Payout to card?
  2. 6.2 Destination rules: must the holder name match the KYC name? Do you verify the account (micro-deposit)? Withdrawals only to accounts previously used for funding?
  3. 6.3 Fees, limits and timing per rail — and a webhook on completion?
  4. 6.4 Large withdrawals: thresholds for extra checks (source of funds)?

One app: self-custody bitcoin plus a cash balance that funds cheap buys delivered straight to the user's own keys. Nothing custodial lingers. The full question list lives in each step — First release scope: steps 0–4 plus withdraw — deposited cash must always have an exit; only sell comes later.
